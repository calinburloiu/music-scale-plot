Martyries of the eight modes

All twenty-one degrees of the three octaves × all eight modes (ehuri / glasuri), every cell filled with an explicit martyria, plus the book's three makam scales as it prints them. Research page for issue #2.

This is the table on p. 83 of the Romanian psaltic theory course, rebuilt with two deliberate differences. The book groups the modes that share a scale into one column („Ehurile 1, 4, 5, 8“) and fills the rest with idem or ca în diatonic; here every mode gets its own column and every cell carries its own martyria, even where that makes four columns identical. And where the book shows one octave, Pă…Pă, this shows all three.

Three columns are different in kind and are ruled off at the right: Muștar, Nișabur and Hisar are makam scales, not modes, and no reference implementation carries them — they are transcribed cell by cell from the book, with each idem and ca în diatonic resolved to the sign it stands for. The book gives them for Pă…Pă only, so the rest of their column is left empty rather than guessed; see Caveats. The book's fourth scale, Agem, is the enharmonic scale of modes III and VII, so it is already among the mode columns.

How each cell is built

A martyria is two SBMuFL glyphs in one string: the note letter (a spacing glyph) followed by the genus sign (a zero-advance mark). The font's mark GPOS lookups stack and centre the second on the first, so no manual positioning is involved — see MARTYRIA-COMPOSITION.md. Hover any cell to see the two glyph names and their codepoints.

Every cell is written out literally in the markup — the two glyphs as numeric character references, e.g. &#xE137;&#xE152; for Πα with the alpha sign, and their names in the cell's title. Nothing on this page is generated at load time, so a wrong or missing sign is corrected by editing that one cell.

Which sign a degree takes depends on the scale, not on the mode's base note, which is why the four diatonic modes share one set of martyries and the two enharmonic ones share another. The per-degree sign tables reproduce those of the Neanes scorewriter (LayoutService.getRootSign), the reference implementation of the notation:

  • Diatonic — a lookup per degree: Πα alpha, Βου legetos, Γα nana, Δι dotted delta, Κε dotted alpha — and the sign changes with the register: Ζω below Πα takes the zo sign, Ζω above Κε takes legetos.
  • Soft chromatic — the two signs alternate by degree parity: Δι and every second degree from it take the soft chromatic Δι sign, the degrees between take the soft chromatic Κε sign.
  • Hard chromatic — the same alternation with the hard chromatic Πα and Δι signs, anchored so that Πα takes the Πα sign.
  • Enharmonic (the agem scale) — the diatonic table with the degrees the two flattenings pull out of the chain re-lettered: the tetrachord below Γα takes the signs of Γα-Δι-Κε (Νη nana, Πα delta, Βου alpha), and a flattened Ζω — the foot of a tetrachord, like Γα — takes nana in place of the diatonic legetos.

Register also decides where the sign goes. Degrees of the low octave carry the …Above marks, so the sign sits on top and the letter underneath; the middle and high octaves carry the …Below marks and read letter-over-sign. That is not a stylistic choice: each note letter declares only one of the two attachment anchors, so the other pairing simply does not stack.

Caveats

  • The genus assignment follows the Romanian tradition the book documents — ehurile 1, 4, 5, 8 diatonic, 2 and 6 chromatic, 3 and 7 enharmonic. Greek practice differs on modes 3 and 7, which it notates diatonically on Γα/Ζω and turns enharmonic with a fthora.
  • Each mode is shown in its principal (sticheraric/papadic) form. Several have secondary forms in another scale — mode 4 heirmologic on Βου (legetos), mode 6 heirmologic soft chromatic, mode 1 tetraphonic from Κε — which would each need a column of their own.
  • The three makam columns stop where the book does. Their other two registers are not derivable from the octave it prints: the diatonic column shows martyries do not simply repeat by octave — Ζω and Νη take a different sign in each register, and Δι and Κε a dotted one only above Κε — so filling those cells would be invention, not transcription.
  • Those columns are also a reading of the book, not a reproduction of it. Each glyph was identified by rendering the candidate signs in this font and comparing them against the scan, and each idem was read as „as in the diatonic column“ rather than „as in the column to the left“ — the reading under which all three come out as coherent scales, each altering the diatonic in one or two places (Muștar at Γα and Δι, Hisar at Δι and Ζω, Nișabur sharing Agem's lower tetrachord with a diatonic upper one).
